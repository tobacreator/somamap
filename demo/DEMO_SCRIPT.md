# SomaMap — demo narration (90–120 seconds)

Spoken script for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon submission.
Track: **Indie developer** (judged on creativity, technical ambition, and whether it works).
Theme: **Build for Your World**.

Read it at a calm, conversational pace. The timings are targets, not cues to rush: the app's interview
takes five to sixteen seconds per question while the model answers, and those pauses are part of the story —
say the next line while the screen is thinking.

---

## 0:00 – 0:14 · The problem (landing page on screen)

> "You know what you feel. Finding the words is another matter."
>
> That is the problem I built SomaMap for. When something feels off, the hard part is often not the feeling —
> it is describing it. Especially if nobody ever gave you the words.

*(Screen: the landing page. Its own words are on screen, so let them carry this beat.)*

---

## 0:14 – 0:30 · What SomaMap is (still landing, scroll a little)

> SomaMap is a vocabulary and journaling tool. It asks a few short questions about how a sensation feels —
> spot or spread, steady or wave-like, what it reminds you of — and helps you put it into words you would
> actually use.
>
> It does not tell you what a sensation means. It has no opinion about that, and you can see that on every
> screen: "SomaMap is a vocabulary and journaling tool. It does not interpret sensations or give medical
> advice."

---

## 0:30 – 1:35 · A real check-in (the core of the demo)

> Let me do a real one. I tap where I feel it — the head — and I describe it in my own words, exactly as I
> would say it out loud.

*(Type a short, plain description, e.g. "heavy, like something pressing down". Then the consent note appears.)*

> Before anything leaves the app, it tells me what will be sent and to whom, and I choose. That is a click,
> not a checkbox buried in settings.

*(Tap Continue with AI. The first question appears.)*

> The model reads what I wrote and chooses which approved question to ask next. Every question's wording
> comes from SomaMap's own reviewed set — the model picks, it does not write the questions. And I can tap a
> word it offers, or ignore it and type my own.

*(Answer the first question. Wait through the next one.)*

> The last question is always the comparison: "What does it reminds you of?" — because that is usually where
> the most useful words are.

*(Answer, then the card appears.)*

---

## 1:35 – 1:55 · The card

> And that is the card. Five fields, nothing else: where, the words I chose, what it feels like, and my own
> sentence — which is the largest thing on the card, because it is the whole point. The app did not add a
> single word of interpretation. There is no field for what it might mean, because there is no such field in
> the schema at all.

---

## 1:55 – 2:20 · The journal (sign in, save, refresh)

> An account is what keeps a card. I sign in, save it, and open the journal.

*(Open the journal. The saved card, the word counts, and the named place are on screen.)*

> There is my card — and underneath it, counts: the words I chose, and the places I named. Those are counts
> of my own words, and the journal says so plainly: "Counted from the words on your own cards, and nothing
> else. SomaMap does not read them."
>
> Watch this — I refresh the page. Still there. It is saved, not remembered by the tab.

---

## 2:20 – 2:35 · Boundaries, as the closing strength

> So: no diagnosis, no interpretation, no advice — and no guessing what any of this means. What SomaMap gives
> you is the thing that is actually hard: your own words, kept somewhere you can find them again.
>
> SomaMap. Find the words for what you feel.

---

## Timing notes

- **Full run: about 2:20.** For a tighter 90–120 second cut, drop the two optional beats below and shorten the
  card and journal narration by a sentence each.
- **Optional beat (cut first):** the consent disclosure sentence at 0:30 — keep the click, lose the line.
- **Optional beat (cut second):** the refresh sentence at 1:55 — the persistence is visible anyway.
- **Do not cut:** the problem (0:00), the model-chooses-the-question line, the five-field card, the word
  counts, and the boundaries close. Those are what the track is judged on: creativity, ambition, and whether
  it works.

## Words to avoid on camera

No medical claims and no clinical language: never say diagnose, treat, symptom, condition, or that SomaMap
"detects" or "identifies" anything. The product's own vocabulary is "sensation", "words", "card", and
"journal" — use those.
